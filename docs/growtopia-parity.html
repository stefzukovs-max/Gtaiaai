The verdict

We built the loop. We did not build the vocabulary.

Punch → seed → splice → lock is the correct spine, and we have it working. What separates Growtopia is not that its loop is better — it's that every block is a small program. A door teleports, a trampoline launches, a provider pays rent, a checkpoint saves your position, a weather machine repaints the sky. Fifty of those behaviours, mixed freely by players, is what makes a world worth visiting.

Three things separate us structurally, in order of how much they cost:

1. The item model. Ours is a hardcoded reg() list with fixed booleans. Theirs is a data table where type is a behaviour id and the renderer, the collision, the drop table and the UI all switch on it. Until we have that, every new block is a code change.

2. Sprite resolution and animation. They author at 32×32 with extra animation frames per tile. We render 16×16 static tiles. That single number is most of the graphical gap.

3. The economy's unit of account. Gems are untradable in Growtopia — the currency players actually price in is the World Lock, and 100 of them fuse into a Diamond Lock. We made gems the currency, which quietly removes the reason to trade at all.

01

How it is actually built

Growtopia was made by two people — Seth Robinson and Mike "Hamumu" Hommel — in C++ against GL/GLES, on Robinson's Proton SDK, which is why it shipped to eight platforms nearly out of the box. The deliberate choice of 2D over 3D was for speed of development and reach onto cheap phones, and it is the reason the art style is what it is.

The numbers that constrain the design

  • Worlds are 100 × 60 tiles. The bottom six rows are unbreakable bedrock, so the usable grid is 100 × 54 — which is exactly the grid we already use.
  • Sprites are authored at 32 × 32. Every tile, every clothing layer, every animation frame lives on one atlas at that resolution.
  • Collision is deliberately trivial. Robinson's postmortem calls it out: simple AABB against the tile grid, no physics engine. "We're not making Limbo here."
  • 3.5 million player worlds in five months, from 400,000 users. The server started single-threaded and had to be rebuilt for 16 cores when concurrency passed 2,000.

The lesson for us is not "add a physics engine." It's that the depth is in the item table, not the simulation. They spent their complexity budget on what a block can be.

The behaviour enum

Every item in Growtopia is a row in items.dat carrying an id, a name, a numeric rarity, a texture coordinate and — the important field — a type. That type is an integer into the behaviour table below, decoded by the modding community. It is the closest thing there is to a design document for the game.

Ten slots, not five

Clothing is TYPE_CLOTHES (20), and the slot is a second field on the item. Growtopia dresses an avatar in ten independent layers; a "set" like Garuda is six or seven items bought separately. We render five.

HatHairFaceNeck ChestShirtPantsFeet BackHandArtifact

Green is what our sprite forge already composites. Hair we draw but do not treat as an equippable item; Shirt, Pants, Neck, Chest, Hand and Artifact do not exist as layers at all. Hand is the interesting omission — it is the slot that holds tools and weapons, and it is what makes a punch animation read as a pickaxe swing instead of a fist.

04

The currency we got wrong

This is the single design decision most worth reversing.

Three currencies, three jobs

  • Gems — soft currency, dropped by breaking blocks and harvesting trees. Cannot be traded or dropped. They exist to buy from the system store, and to be a sink that never inflates the player economy.
  • World Locks — a real item, tradeable, and therefore the unit players quote prices in. Tap 100 in your inventory and they fuse into a Diamond Lock. The currency is a thing you can also use, which is why it holds value.
  • Growtokens — premium, non-tradable, spendable only in the Growtoken Store.

Our build makes gems tradeable and makes the World Lock a shop item bought with gems. That inverts the whole thing: it means the fastest route to any cosmetic is grinding dirt, and no player ever needs another player. Flipping this — gems untradable, locks as the trade unit — restores the reason the game is an MMO.

And the thing that prints money

TYPE_PROVIDER (38) is a block you place that yields a harvest on a timer, typically daily. It is Growtopia's answer to idle income and the backbone of most players' economy. We have nothing equivalent — our only income is active mining.
