The edges belong to the HUD

Identity top-left, world identity top-centre, crowd metrics top-right. Nothing floats over the middle third except a trade request, which is the one interruption worth blocking play for.

Signage is the player's voice

Neon is a placeable block, not a decal. Every glowing letter on this screen was spelled out by the owner, one tile at a time — which is why no two worlds look alike.

Chat sits where the eye rests

Left rail, bottom-anchored, three tabs deep. Speech bubbles also float over the speaker so newcomers can follow a conversation without ever reading the log.

Plate 02

Avatar Lineup

Eight silhouettes that have to read at 32 pixels tall across a crowded world. Identity is carried by shape first, glow second, colour third — you should know who is standing on the other side of the shop before you can see their face.

TurnaroundFront · back · and the same body with everything stripped off — every avatar is one base rig wearing eight layers.
Plate 03

The Cosmetics Vault

Thirty-eight launch pieces across five slots. Rarity is not a stat — it is pure supply. A Mythic pair of wings does nothing a Common pair doesn't, which is exactly why people will trade a world's worth of gems for them.

Wings are the flex slot

Biggest silhouette change, biggest drop rate gap. They render behind the body and are the only cosmetic with an idle animation of their own, so a rare pair is visible even when the wearer is standing still.

Eyes are the cheap thrill

Six pixels of real estate, and players will grind a week for the right six. Sold in the starter shop for gems so every new account has one thing worth showing off on day one.

Nothing is bind-on-pickup

Every piece in this vault can cross the trade window. The economy is the endgame; a cosmetic that can't be sold is a cosmetic nobody chases.

Three Systems, One Grid

Chat, trade and inventory are the whole social contract. They share one slot component, one rarity border language and one 4px rhythm, so a player who learns to read an inventory can read a trade window without being taught twice.

Plate 05

Skyblock Hub

The one world nobody owns. Every session lands here, and every portal on the platform is a real player's build that earned its place on the board this week. The hub is a leaderboard you can walk around in.

Featured is earned, not sold

Portal placement is driven by unique visitors and time-on-world over a rolling seven days. There is no way to buy the centre arch, and everyone can see the numbers that put a world there.

The hub teaches the game

A new player walks past a shop, a parkour tower, a casino and a dance floor in fifteen seconds. Nobody has to be told what to build — they have already seen four answers.

Crowds are the lighting

Busy worlds glow brighter. Portal bloom scales with live player count, so the hub visibly breathes across a day without a single UI number.

Plate 06

Production Rules

The constraints that keep ten thousand player-built worlds looking like one game. Break these and the world reads as a mod pack.

Palette

Twilight neon

Grounds are violet-biased near-blacks, never neutral grey — the world is lit by signage, and signage tints its own shadows. Neon hues are used at full saturation only as emitted light: a glowing block, a sign, a portal, a rarity border. Nothing that isn't a light source gets to be that bright.

Sprite anatomy

24 × 32 px
  1. Wings — behind, own idle cycle
  2. Cape — behind, sways on move
  3. Body — skin, hands, base rig
  4. Legs & shoes — recolour layer
  5. Outerwear — torso and sleeves
  6. Face — six pixels of eyes
  7. Hair — sits under headwear
  8. Headwear — overrides hair top

Block set

16 × 16 px · seamless on all four edges

Every block is authored as a tile that tessellates without a visible seam, with all detail inside the middle 12 pixels so adjacent blocks never fight. Emissive blocks carry their glow in a second pass, which is what lets a player spell a shop name in light.

Readability beats detail

Any sprite that stops reading at 100% zoom on a phone gets simplified, not shaded harder. Two tones and a rim light will out-perform four tones every time at this size.

Glow is a budget

One emissive focal point per screen third. When a player's build blows the budget the world still holds together, because nothing in the base tileset competes.

Every cosmetic is one layer

No cosmetic may span two equip layers or require a bespoke body. If a concept can't be drawn inside its own layer, it ships as two pieces or it doesn't ship.
